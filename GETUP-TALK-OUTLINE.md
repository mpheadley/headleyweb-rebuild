"How local business owners actually show up when someone searches for what they do — Google Maps, AI search, the whole picture — and what's in their control."

---

## Core insight (the talk's spine)

Most local business owners think their website is a brochure. It's actually a ranking signal. The businesses showing up in Google Maps and AI search results aren't there because they spent more money — they're there because their site answers the right questions, loads fast on a phone, and tells Google exactly where they are and what they do.

---

## Talk structure (60–75 minutes)

### 1. The map pack demo (10 min)
Open Google. Search "best HVAC in Anniston" or "[their trade] in [their city]."
- Show how three businesses dominate the results
- Walk through what those three businesses have in common
- Ask the room: "How many of you have Googled your own business name this week?"
- Most haven't. This is their first surprise.

**Key point:** Which business shows up has almost nothing to do with who does better work or who's been around longer. It comes down to site speed, Google Business Profile activity, and whether the site answers the questions people actually type.

### 2. The AI search demo (10 min)
Open ChatGPT. Type "pipes leaking in Anniston" or "my AC stopped working in [city]."
- Show the AI answer — no business names, just information
- Then show a search that *does* return business recommendations
- Walk through what those businesses did to get cited

**Key point:** AI answers the question before anyone sees a business listing. The businesses recommended are the ones whose sites are built to be cited. Most aren't — yet. This is an early-adopter window.

### 3. The three things in their control (20 min)
Frame as a checklist they can act on this week.

**Step 1 — Does your site load fast on a phone?**
- Pull up Google PageSpeed Insights (free tool)
- Have them run their own site live in the room
- Show what a failing score looks like vs. passing

**Step 2 — Is your Google Business Profile active?**
- Recent photos, recent posts, owner responses to reviews
- Show a dormant profile vs. an active one side by side
- Recent activity signals to Google that the business is still open and engaged

**Step 3 — Does your site answer the questions people actually type?**
- Not "we provide quality service" — but "emergency plumber Anniston" or "HVAC repair Oxford AL after hours"
- Show how to find the actual questions using Google's autocomplete and "People also ask"

### 4. The before/after (10 min)
Bring real screenshots — a site before and after a rebuild.
- Side-by-side mobile screenshots
- Before: slow, unclear, no local terms
- After: fast, answers real questions, Google Business Profile integrated

**Note:** Replace with Between Worlds or H3 data when 30-day results are available.

### 5. What to do this week (5 min)
Give them three actions they can take before the next session:
1. Run their site on PageSpeed Insights (free, 2 minutes)
2. Log into their Google Business Profile and post one update
3. Google their own trade + city and write down what the top result says that theirs doesn't

### Q&A (10–15 min)

## Tone notes

This is education, not a pitch. The room is full of people who could become clients — but only if they trust you first. The talk's job is to hand them a tool, not sell a service. Credibility comes from specificity: real demos, real screenshots, real numbers. No jargon, no upsell language.

The Adam Harper contrast: he leads with urgency and stats. Lead with understanding. Same material, different frame.
