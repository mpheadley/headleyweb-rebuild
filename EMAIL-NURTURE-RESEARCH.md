# Email Nurture Research — StoryBrand Best Practices

> Researched April 2026. Applied to `EMAIL-NURTURE-DRAFTS.md`.

---

## Key Findings

### Structure
- The 5-part SB nurture arc (welcome → problem → plan → success → failure) is confirmed as the standard framework from *Marketing Made Simple* by Donald Miller.
- Each email should follow: bring up a problem → lay out a plan → explain how life improves → offer a picture of success → sprinkle in failure stakes.

### Format
- **Plain text outperforms HTML** for personal nurture sequences. HTML signals "marketing blast" before the first word is read.
- **One CTA per email.** Multiple choices paralyze readers.
- **80/20 rule:** 80% value, 20% ask across the full sequence.

### Timing
- SB best practice: one email per week over 4–6 weeks.
- Hitting a new lead twice in the first week (Days 0 + 3) increases unsubscribe risk before trust is established.
- Adjusted sequence to weekly cadence: Days 0, 7, 14, 21, 28.

### Triggered sequences
- Behavior-triggered emails (contact added to audience) get ~30% more opens and ~50% higher clicks vs. broadcast newsletters.
- Confirms Resend Automations approach over a scheduled broadcast.

### Guide positioning
- SB frames the brand as the "forever guide" — not just a one-time vendor.
- Authority should be established early: specific industries, geographies, or results. Vague experience claims ("I've built enough of these") are weaker than concrete ones ("I've rebuilt sites for HVAC, landscaping, and transportation businesses across Northeast Alabama").

### Gaps identified in original sequence
| Gap | Fix applied |
|---|---|
| Authority vague in Email 1 | Added specific industries + geography |
| Villain implied, not named in Email 2 | Added "Right now, that's probably not you" |
| Email 4 had no CTA | Added reply ask |
| Email 4 opened apologetically | Reframed to confident honest explanation |
| Day 0 → Day 3 too fast | Adjusted to weekly cadence |
