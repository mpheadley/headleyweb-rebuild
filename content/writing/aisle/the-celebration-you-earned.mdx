The vow renewal is not a redo. It is the celebration you earned.

Standard vows fit a beginning. Custom vows fit a history. The ring they bought at 23 is not the ring they want at 40. Most couples who renew their vows write them from scratch — not because the originals were wrong, but because they have something more specific to say now.

## What the numbers say

38% of couples who renew their vows do it at the ten-year mark. It is the single most common milestone. Half the rest choose their own timing — after something hard, after something good, or simply because they decided to.

73% write their own vows the second time. 77% use the renewal to upgrade their rings. 68% keep it to close family and friends: smaller list, higher spend per person, more of what actually matters.

## Who this is for

**The Missed Milestone.** COVID. A deployment. A courthouse signing with ten people and a phone. You did what you had to do. The celebration you planned is still worth having.

**The Anniversary.** Ten years. Twenty-five. Fifty. A milestone that deserves more than a dinner reservation. Your people, your story, the photos your kids will keep.

**The Fresh Start.** You came through something hard. Illness, loss, a stretch of years that tested everything. The renewal says: we made it. And we choose this again.

## What Eastern Alabama can offer

This is not a destination wedding. It is a homecoming.

The venues here are real places — museum gardens, historic chapels, farm property, downtown spaces that have been part of this community for a long time. The officiants have married people in this county for decades. The florists know what grows here in October.

A vow renewal in Eastern Alabama means something. It happens in the same county where you built your life. Your guests don't have to fly anywhere. The people in the photos know the people in the room.

That is worth something.
