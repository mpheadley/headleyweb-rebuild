## The clinical thread — what may have triggered it

Matt recovered from his first depressive episode on nortriptyline, a first-generation tricyclic antidepressant. It made his heart race — he was prescribed propranolol (a beta blocker) to manage that side effect. He was eager to get off nortriptyline. He may have titrated off too fast.

His psychiatrist's retrospective read: the fast taper may have triggered the mania. Or the bipolar was just there all along — the antidepressant, the taper, the illness were accelerants, not causes.

Both things can be true. You can't always disentangle them. That uncertainty is part of the story — the question "when did it start" doesn't have a clean answer, and his psychiatrist knows it.

**Note:** Antidepressant-induced mania in undiagnosed bipolar is clinically documented. Nortriptyline (TCA) carries this risk. The fast taper is a separate possible trigger. The back-to-back flu and COVID in late January adds a third. The bipolar as underlying condition that was always there is the fourth. All four may be true simultaneously.

## The all-night reorganization (Matt, Apr 20 2026)

One night: reorganized the entire kitchen — drawers, plates, food. Same night: reorganized the desk with the desktop computer, the TV stand with video games, movies, cables. All night. Watching Monty Python and the Holy Grail while doing it. Left sticky notes on every cabinet door and drawer labeling what was where.

The sticky notes are the detail. He didn't just reorganize — he documented the reorganization, built a system, labeled the system. The labor wasn't enough; it had to be annotated. That's the manic mind: not just doing, but making the doing legible, permanent, systematized. At 3am. With Monty Python on in the background.

## The physical architecture of the episode (Matt, Apr 20 2026)

9 or 10pm: went to bed in earnest, after working in earnest up until then.
11pm or midnight: woke up flooded with ideas.
12am–4am: lying in the bathtub. Editing Facebook reels. Chatting with ChatGPT. This happened on quite a few occasions.
Morning: on the road exploring opportunities, passing out business cards, hustling on the farm.
2pm: napping — after struggling to stay awake on short drives in the afternoon.

The bathtub is the detail. Not the desk, not the couch. The bathtub at midnight. The place you go to be still becomes the place you go to work when you can't stop working.

The dozing on short drives wasn't laziness — it was the body failing after the night before. He was running the day on a few hours of sleep taken in the wrong places, at the wrong times, for weeks.

## When it peaked

Late January 2025 — after Matt and Heather had back-to-back flu and COVID. The illness is likely the physiological trigger: sleep deprivation, fever, the body under sustained stress, coming out the other side. The mania that had been building since fall 2024 (possibly earlier) crested here.

Specific scene from that period: preaching at Anniston First with Tammy present. Got choked up on stage. Speaking rapidly. The emotion and the pace are the mania visible in real time — performing at the front of a church, in front of the person he was most managed around, unable to hold the pace down.
