# The Distance Between Neighbors

**Status:** Seed — one moment, one thread to pull

**Origin:** April 16, 2026. Lucy Morris texted after reading the Aquality Farm piece. She said: "I had no clue about Aquality Farm." Lucy lives nearby. The farm is not hidden. That gap between physical proximity and actual awareness is the thing.

---

## The Seed

Proximity doesn't guarantee connection. People live five miles from something — a farm, a ministry, a family — and have no idea it exists. SL exists partly because of that gap. The profiles are a corrective: *here is what is happening near you.*

This connects to the interfaith piece. April Lafolette's work on reconciliation is happening in the same county where people don't know their neighbors across racial or faith lines. The geography is shared. The distance is something else.

## Open question before writing

Is this Matt's story or the idea's story? There's a version where Matt walks through his own ignorance — things he didn't know about neighbors during the farm years, when he was too deep in his own crisis to see out. That's more personal and more interesting than the abstract version.

---

## Raw material — Matt's words (April 16, 2026)

What he was missing during the farm years, the 16-20 hour days, before everything tipped:

- Heather was struggling. With fear about him. With depression. She got so frustrated with him — he could barely stop to listen to her.
- He had anger flare-ups (part of mania) that scared her and the kids.
- He also laughed. A lot. A lot a lot.
- Other people were struggling with bipolar — he didn't know.
- Some of his church coworkers and community were concerned. He brushed it off.

**The detail that matters:** The laughing. Not the anger — the laughing. Because from the outside, someone laughing a lot doesn't look like someone who's unreachable. It looks like someone who's fine. Better than fine. Heather is scared. The kids are scared. And he's laughing.

That's the distance. Not miles. Not distraction. A person right there, lit up, completely gone.

**The real piece:** Not about neighbors. About how mania narrows your field of vision until the people closest to you become the people you see least clearly. Lucy not knowing about Aquality Farm is just the door.

**What Heather needed:** For him to stop. Stop going on 30-minute errands that took all day — out passing out business cards, looking for urban farm plots, striking up conversations. She needed him to come home. He couldn't.
