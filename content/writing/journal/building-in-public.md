## The pivot

What I had, underneath the rubble, was a set of skills I'd been using for years without thinking of them as a career. I'd built websites for my church and our flower farm. I understood how to make something clear, functional, and honest — how to take what a person or organization actually does and put it in front of the people who need to find it. I just hadn't called it a profession yet.

So I started Headley Web & SEO. Not with a grand launch or a marketing budget, but with the kind of stubborn, one-foot-in-front-of-the-other energy that comes from having no other option.

I'm good at it. Not because I went to school for it, but because nineteen years of ministry taught me how to listen to what people actually need, and four years of homesteading taught me that nothing grows without patience and consistent work. The technical skills are real, but the instinct for people is what makes the sites work.
