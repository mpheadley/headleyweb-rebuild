## Raw material — Matt's framing (April 25, 2026)

The pastoral ministry parallels with the farmers market manager role are real. Worth holding the tension between similar and different rather than collapsing into either.

### Similar

- You show up weekly and the community comes to you — same rhythm as Sunday morning
- You know everyone's name and their story. You're the connective tissue, not the product
- You're managing relationships, not transactions — a vendor who has a bad week needs pastoral instinct, not policy enforcement
- Student workers reporting to you = young people in your orbit, same formation dynamic
- The market has regulars who become family over a season

### Different

- No one is in crisis at the farmers market (usually). Lower emotional weight
- No expectation of spiritual care — you can give it freely without it being the job
- Clear clock-in/clock-out. The role ends at 1 PM Saturday
- You're not the center. The vendors and buyers are. You're the infrastructure
- The church asked you to carry things pastors carry. This job doesn't

### The line that lands

> The biggest pastoral carryover is probably this: you already know how to hold a community. You've done it from a stage, in hospital rooms, at gravesides. Managing a farmers market is a lighter version of the same instinct — and this time you don't have to be available at 2 AM.

That sentence is the center of gravity. Save it. Build the piece around it.

---

## What the piece is actually about

Not "I took a farmers market job." That's news, not writing.

The piece hiding here: **what it feels like to step into a pastoral role that doesn't ask anything pastoral of you.** The relief of that. The permission to hold space without carrying the grief. A lighter version of the same instinct.

That's the angle. Faith-shaped attention applied to something secular. The instinct survives the institution.

---

## Structure for the finished piece

Right now this is observation-first (parallel lists, then a closing thought). Published version should be **scene-first**:

1. **Open with a specific Saturday.** A vendor's bad week. A regular who lingers because they need someone to talk to. A student worker arriving early. Concrete moment, sensory, anchored in time and place.
2. **Pivot to the recognition.** *Oh — I've done this before. Different building. Same instinct.*
3. **Then the parallels.** But weave them through the scene, not as a list. Show, don't catalog.
4. **Land on the difference that matters.** Not "no 2am calls" as throwaway — the actual weight of being able to give pastoral attention without being asked to carry pastoral burden. That distinction is the whole piece.
