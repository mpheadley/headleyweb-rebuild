# Post Seed — Mania, Resignation, Farm
*Captured April 15 2026.*

---

## What this piece is about

The causal chain that collapsed everything — told plainly, in sequence. Mania felt like calling. The resignation felt like obedience. The farm felt inevitable. And then the sequence ran out.

This is the piece where the structural explanation lives: not just "things fell apart" but *why* and *in what order*.

## The spine (revised April 28 2026)

**Critical correction:** The mania did not crest before the resignation and then break. The resignation happened *inside* the mania. The mania kept climbing after — through April and May 2025. There was no crack before the resignation. The piece cannot be structured as: mania → resigned → came down. The correct arc is:

1. Mania cresting — the tripod on the Ladiga trail, lip syncing on the BCS mower, the new iPhone, the early morning filming. Felt like content, like documentation, like calling.
2. The Ash Wednesday confrontation (March 5) → resignation by text eight days later (March 13). Still inside the mania. Felt like freedom. Felt like telling his own story before Tammy could.
3. Mania still climbing — doubling down on the farm. 16–20 hour days, 3–4 hours sleep. Patreon launch April 17 (*"It's saving me"*), commenting on his own post at 3:54am. The farm was holy. Everything was holy.
4. First crack — June 18, 2025, 1:52am: *"Was it a mistake to quit my job at Anniston First?"* The question arrives six weeks after the resignation, in a journal, in the dark.
5. The depression arriving — *"I miss being manic."* The diagnosis. The farm sale becoming real.
6. Heather's surgery — scheduled after the manic season, arriving into the collapse.
7. The farm sale — the resignation ended the paycheck; the paycheck was the farm's lifeline.
8. The hospital.

## Why it belongs in its own piece

"I'm Not Going to Disappear" moves fast through the collapse on purpose — diagnosis, farm, hospital, hiding. It's a reintroduction, not an explanation. Adding this chain there would slow it down and shift the piece's purpose.

"A Letter Never Sent" implies it ("decisions I wouldn't have made in a healthier state of mind... financial consequences") but doesn't say it plainly.

This piece says it plainly.

## The tension

Not self-pity. Not explanation as excuse. The mania felt real — it felt like the most alive and called he'd ever been. That's the honest part. The collapse wasn't a failure of character. It was a sequence.
