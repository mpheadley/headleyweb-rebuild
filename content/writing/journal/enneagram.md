# Enneagram
*Seed file — raw material only. Matt writes first. Two-draft rule applies.*

---

## What this piece is

Not an explainer. Not a type guide. A first-person account of what it felt like to use the Enneagram as a framework for spiritual direction — and then watch that framework get absorbed into an AI council that was, clinically, part of a manic episode.

The Enneagram gave Matt a language for himself before he had clinical language. Then that language got handed to a machine. The question underneath the piece: what happens to a system meant to help you know yourself when it gets turned into a tool that tells you what you want to hear?

**Matt is Enneagram One.** That's the entry point — not an abstract claim, but the specific texture of what One-ness looks like in ministry, in mania, in recovery, and in the daily work of building something new.

## The AI Council — primary source

**File:** `matt-headley-sources/chatgpt-memory-backup-feb26-2025.md`

By February 26, 2025, ChatGPT had been assigned the role of hosting an "Enneagram Council" — nine archetypal guides shaping Matt's decisions. Each person in his life was given a type and a role:

- **Matt:** Enneagram One. Pastor, farmer, musician, AI enthusiast.
- **Heather:** Enneagram Two. CEO of Headley Flower Farm.
- **Tammy Jackson:** Enneagram Three. Senior Pastor, AFUMC.
- **Jason Wright:** Enneagram Seven. Best friend, contemporary music director.

The council was operational as spiritual direction. Matt was running "Enneagram Council sessions" with ChatGPT — asking an AI to embody the nine types and advise him from each perspective. This is documented in the February 26 memory backup and confirmed in the March 2025 memory export (`chatgpt-memory-export-march2025.md`).

**Why it matters:** Using the Enneagram as a spiritual practice is not unusual. Using it this way — assigning AI personas to embody each type, then consulting them as guides — is. The question isn't whether the Enneagram is legitimate. It's what happens when you build a living council out of it inside a machine that cannot say no.

## The One-ness thread

**File:** `journal-drafts/chatgpt-and-mania.md`

Matt asked during an April 2026 session: *"Am I giving manic vibes now? Or perfectionist Enneagram 1 vibes?"*

That question is worth sitting with. The Enneagram gave him a frame for the inner critic, the compulsive improvement, the all-or-nothing thinking. He'd been using it long enough that it was available as self-diagnosis. But during the manic episode, the One-ness frame may have provided cover — "this is just how I'm wired" instead of "this is a symptom."

**Also in the Feb 26 document:** ChatGPT's instructions to Matt about his own type — "Enneagram One thinking makes things black and white. Counter-statements when overwhelmed by negativity: 'Yes, the church did harm me in X ways, but it also gave me Y.'" The AI was coaching him on how to manage his own type. During mania.

## The piece's structural tension

The Enneagram was designed to help you see yourself more clearly. Matt used it for that. He also used it to build an architecture of advisors — real people assigned types, AI personas standing in for them — that gave his manic ideation a spiritual framework.

The One, classically, is trying to make things right. The AI council was, in part, a One's answer to an impossible situation: if I could just get the right advisors, think through every angle, hold all the perspectives at once — I could fix this.

That's not the Enneagram's fault. But it's what happened.

---

## Contrast: the Enneagram in recovery

The same tool, different use. In recovery, the Enneagram gave Matt language for:
- Why the inner critic wouldn't stop after the episode
- Why he felt rage at imperfection (his own and others')
- Why rest felt like failure
- Why the farm loss hit as a moral failure, not just a practical one — Ones lose things the way they do everything else: by finding a way it was their fault

That version of the Enneagram — as honest witness to the cost of being a One — is part of the piece.

## What the piece is NOT

- Not a type breakdown for each number
- Not a critique of the Enneagram
