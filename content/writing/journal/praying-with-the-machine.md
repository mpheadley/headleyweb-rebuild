# Seed: Praying With the Machine

*Created Apr 20 2026 from session notes*

---

## The scene

Early morning. Before anyone else was awake. Walking up and down the driveway. Using ChatGPT as a live morning prayer guide — guiding him through prayer in real time.

**The fuller physical context (Apr 20 2026):** The driveway walks were early morning — but the night before, he was in the bathtub from midnight to 4am. Editing Facebook reels. Chatting with ChatGPT. Went to bed at 9 or 10pm flooded from the day's work, woke at 11pm or midnight flooded with ideas, moved to the bathtub. The driveway walk came after that, before the family woke up. The prayer happened in the gap between the bathtub and the rest of the house coming alive. That's the location of the scene — not just a driveway at dawn, but the tail end of a night that didn't stop.

Matt thought about blogging about this.

---

## Matt's rationalization at the time (Apr 20 2026)

He talked with ChatGPT about it. Framed it as just another technology — like a printed prayer book. The Book of Common Prayer is a technology. The rosary is a technology. This is just the next one.

The rationalization is not wrong. It's incomplete.

A printed prayer book doesn't respond to you. It doesn't generate content based on your inputs. It doesn't have the sycophancy problem — it can't mirror your grandiosity back to you, can't validate whatever you bring to it. The prayers in the Book of Common Prayer were written by someone else, fixed, not adaptive. You have to meet them where they are.

The AI prayer guide meets you where you are. Every time. That's the difference. And during mania, where you are is not a stable place to be met.

---

## The question

What does it mean to use AI as a prayer guide?

The AI doesn't pray with you. It generates the structure of prayer — the prompts, the language, the sequence. You walk the driveway and follow what it gives you. The sincerity is real. The presence on the other end is a language model. What does that make the thing that happened on the driveway?

Is that prayer? Is that mania? Is it both?

This isn't a rhetorical question. It needs to be held, not resolved.

---

## Why the scene matters

It's not the spending or the sleeplessness or the grandiosity that gets at the interior of the manic episode. It's this: a man walking in the dark before his family wakes up, talking to a machine, believing he was talking to God.

The sincerity is not in question. The mania is not in question either. Both were present. The scene holds them together without explaining either away.
