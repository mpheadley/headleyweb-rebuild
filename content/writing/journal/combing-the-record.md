*Created Apr 20 2026 from session notes*

---

## What this piece is

The process itself — going back through voice memos, texts, emails, Patreon posts, trying to locate when the mania started and what it looked like. The archaeology of a year you can't fully trust. What it's like to be the investigator and the evidence at the same time.

Matt's words (Apr 20 2026):
> "maybe a seed is this process im going through right now, combing and sifting through artifacts like voice memos and emails."

This session is itself a primary source. Matt spent a morning pulling voice memos off a machine, transcribing them in real time, reading them for the first time in some cases, asking what they mean. That's the piece — or part of it. The reading-my-manic-writing.md and this one may be the same piece from two different angles (the artifact vs. the session of reading it).

---

## The AI-assisted archaeology problem

Matt is using AI to comb through AI-enabled mania. Claude is transcribing the voice memos, surfacing the patterns, flagging the key lines. There's something worth naming there — the tool that amplified the episode is now the tool helping reconstruct it. Is that recovery or recursion?

He asked directly: *"i even wonder if ai syncophancy is affecting me now. maybe even ai psychosis? spending most days at home building and chatting with ai for 8 hours or more."*

That question is not rhetorical. It belongs in the piece.

## The Facebook comments question

When Matt shared publicly on Facebook for the first time after recovering from his first depressive episode, comments came in. Those comments made him first wonder if he could write a book. He notes: *"I may have been manic then."*

The moment when other people's responses to your writing make you think you're a writer — and then you have to ask: was that the writing, or was it the mania, or both? That question is not answerable. That's why it belongs in the piece.

## The session itself as artifact (Apr 20 2026)

Much of this session happened on a Monday morning while the family was getting ready for school. Matt had been slipping away constantly over the past few weeks — Heather getting irritated. He was surprised by the volume of artifacts that surfaced.

The parallel is not lost: absorbed in reconstructing a manic episode, while the family moves around him in another room, while Heather gets irritated. The difference from the mania is that he noticed it, named it, and stopped.

That noticing is the third act of every piece in this cluster. Not recovery as triumphant arrival. Recovery as the moment you catch yourself and come back.

They left for school and work on time. Nobody missed anything. That's different from the mania too.

## Is this a standalone piece?

Yes. The structure is already there: you don't just *read* the record — you have to find it first. Some is locked behind an email. Some didn't sync. Some is 0 bytes. The act of looking is itself a thing that happened. The Apr 19 4am recording that exists as a stub — 0 bytes, phone-only — is as much evidence as the recordings that survived.

Guardrail: every move toward "the process of finding" has to land in something specific that was actually found. No pure meta. The absence earns its place alongside the presence.
