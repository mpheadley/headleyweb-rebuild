# After the Acute Phase
*Journal entry seed*

---

## The piece

The moment you realize you're not in crisis anymore and don't know what to do with that. Nobody writes about the weird flatness after the acute phase ends.

---

## Matt's words (April 19 2026)

"will the material, the life after the acute crisis get boring?"

"will i run out of things to say? will this journal dry up after the good stuff is written about? what else will i write about? bipolar, living with it? rebuilding? my web design business? will that be interesting? will i consider reentering ministry after this and write about that?"

---

## Notes

- The question itself is the piece — not "will life get boring" but what it feels like when the emergency is over and you're just living
- Crisis writing has permission built in. This piece earns that permission a different way: by being honest about the flatness
- The thing underneath: most people can't write about ordinary life honestly. The crisis gave Matt that muscle. He doesn't lose it when the crisis ends.
- Possible images: a Sunday morning at home instead of leading worship, six months in. A client meeting where he notices he's doing the same thing he did as a pastor. One of the kids asking about the farm.
- Don't write this until it's actually being lived — the question is too hypothetical right now. Come back to it.
