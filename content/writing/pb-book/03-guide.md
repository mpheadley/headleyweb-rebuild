## Opening

September on the BCS foil mower.

Middle of the day. The kind of heat that runs through a cotton shirt before the first pass of the field is done. I was behind the mower, the noise of it in my chest, the smell of cut grass and red Alabama clay.

I noticed I was working. Not pretending to work. Actually working. For the first time in months.

I had not put a name on it yet. I just felt it in my body.

Victory. Hope. Energized.

---

A few months before the mower morning, I was sitting in Dr. Hadley's office with Heather.

It was June 2024. I was telling her what was going on. This was an emergency. Our stability, our income, the whole shape of our family was tied to me getting through this. I needed something to work.

"You will get better," she said.

She added: usually we have to try different kinds of solutions.

I was worried there was not enough time.

Heather cried.

We started nortriptyline.

---

I had not wanted to be the one who needed it.

For most of my life I had believed that faith and willpower could carry me through anything. That accepting psychiatric medication would be a kind of giving up. I had watched other people take SSRIs and quietly thought to myself that I would not be one of them.

A friend put it best when she said, "Yes, my serotonin is store-bought!"

That shifted something. Not the depression. The story I was telling myself about the depression.

The diagnosis I had at the time was clinical depression. The bipolar diagnosis was over a year away. The hospitalization was over a year away. I did not know any of that yet. I just knew that something was wrong and the things that had always worked were not working.

---

Healing was not instant. It was slow, with small moments of light breaking through.

A few Saturdays before we went to the beach, I was out on the BCS. Middle of the day. Sweating. Working hard. The flower field needed mowing and I was the one mowing it, and I noticed that I was the one mowing it. I was driven. Productive. The first time in months.

Victory. Hope. Energized.

I did not name it yet as the medication working. I named it as the feeling.

---

That September we drove to Tybee Island.

I had gotten poison ivy on my eye while we were setting up the sheep irrigation. The kids were with us. I was body surfing in the Gulf, which I had not been able to do the year before because the year before I could barely get out of bed.

Heather looked at me at some point during that week and saw that I was back. That was when she knew the medication was working. Not when the doctor said it would. When the man in the water came up laughing.

---

This is the part of the story most business books skip.

You read a book about clarity, about messaging, about brand voice — and the writer is always already there. Already clear, already confident, already on the other side of whatever broke. They tell you how to fix it without ever naming the thing that had to break first.

But the framework in this book did not come from a workshop. It came from after Dr. Hadley's office. After the BCS in September. After Tybee. After I had been the one in the room who needed something to work and was not sure it would.

I do not know exactly which client this book is for. But if you are the one whose business is the wound and whose recovery is also the work — this part is for you.

The guide is the one who has been through it.

---

## Closing

That fall the kids and I drove to Tybee Island. Heather met us there.

The water was warm. I body-surfed in the Gulf for the first time since the year before. I came up out of the foam laughing.

Heather looked at me.
