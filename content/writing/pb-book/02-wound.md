I was sitting in my office watching a video of James Finley talking about clinical depression versus the dark night of the soul.

Through the window behind me, the light came in from the wrong side of the day. Hallway in front of me with the other staff's offices. I was facing the hallway and the screen.

The question landed before the answer did. Is this what I am experiencing? Could that be clinical depression?

Fear. Dread. A knowing I did not want to own.

I left work that day. Things got worse after.

---

I reached out for a therapist.

It took months. The one I had been working with was not available. She had been making me wait for hours in our last sessions. I would arrive at her office, sit in the waiting room for two hours, and then she would come out and tell me she was dealing with her phone bill, or her insurance bill, or something else that had to be handled before I could be seen.

I stopped going.

---

## [MATT WRITES — the search for help]

*Spring–summer 2024. The next part of the search. A psychologist. A psychiatrist. A friend. A family member. Someone who pointed you somewhere. Maybe somewhere that worked, maybe somewhere that didn't. One short paragraph, no names yet. The pattern is the thing — that you kept looking even when nothing was landing.*

*Note: the mushroom arc and the named psychologist are held in `flags.md` for your decision. If you choose to include them, this paragraph is where they go. If you choose to anonymize, this is where the anonymized version goes. If you choose to leave them out of the book entirely, this paragraph still works as "I tried things that did not help."*

---

I had believed for most of my life that faith and willpower could carry me through anything. That accepting psychiatric medication would be a kind of giving up. I had watched other people take SSRIs and quietly thought to myself that I would not be one of them.

A friend put it best when she said, *"Yes, my serotonin is store-bought!"*

That shifted something. Not the depression. The story I was telling myself about the depression.

---

The wilderness was not just a metaphor.

I went on hikes during that season looking for something the church and the office could not give me. Foggy woods. Cold mornings. A pastor in a clergy robe on New Year's Eve trying to lead worship while the bottom kept going. I went looking for peace and did not find it.

I sat by the pool at Camp Lee on Sunday afternoons surrounded by friends and family, completely disconnected.

I tried everything. Counseling, prayer, yoga, hiking, reading. None of it lifted the fog the way it had before.

---

I wrestled with God in my journal.

I dumped my anger and grief and frustration onto the page, shouting *Where are you, God?* into what felt like a void. I doubted any of it was being heard.

Psalm 139 had always been a comfort. *Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can I flee from your presence?* During that season the words felt like a challenge. The line about God's presence felt absent the most when I needed it most.

I held onto the verse anyway.

---

## [MATT WRITES — closing]

*Land on an image from the lowest point. Not the recovery. Not the diagnosis. The bottom. Could be the hike with nobody on it, the moment at the Camp Lee pool when everything was loud but you couldn't hear it, a Sunday morning when the words came out of your mouth and meant nothing to you. One paragraph. Don't explain. End on the image. The next chapter — Mask — will begin the climb out, the wrong way.*
