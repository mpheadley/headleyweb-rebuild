# Chapter 1 — Actor

*First-pass compile from voice session 2026-05-21 + published SL essays + journal-drafts. Matt writes opening and closing paragraphs. All scene material is verbatim from Matt's transcripts or paraphrased from his own published prose.*
*Last updated: 2026-05-21.*

## Opening

Six-thirty in the morning at the Anniston Farmers Market. Spring 2023.

The truck doors open. Buckets in both hands. The kids with us, bleary-eyed, helping carry. Heather already setting up the table.

Bright colors out of the buckets. Whatever was hitting that week.

The other vendors started turning to look as we set up.

*Wow.*

That word, three or four times, from three or four different stalls. *Oh my gosh. Look at that. Who are these people?*

I felt the pride. I stood tall.

---

The farm sat on a slope on Ridgewood Avenue. A little white and green house at the top. A high tunnel and a flower field below. We bought it in 2021. Same year I started at First Methodist Anniston, four miles south. Two new starts in the same season.

By spring 2023, the farm was working. The BCS tractor. The high tunnel humming. Heather growing flowers. Me selling them.

Saturday mornings at the Anniston market, we'd pull up with buckets. People turned to look as we brought them out. *Wow*. The other vendors leaned over. *Oh my gosh. Look at that. Who are these people?* We were a flagship vendor before long.

Six-thirty in the morning. The kids with us, bleary-eyed. Very colorful spring color.

I felt the pride. I could stand tall.

---

I had stood tall in one other place.

A Sunday at First Methodist, somewhere in the first year. They had told me when they hired me I was being brought on as a pastor. A step up from Weaver, where I had been the solo pastor for six and a half years. Once I got there, the role got reframed. Music director. Staff. Not pastoral. The first conversation about a raise went to the personnel chair. The answer was no.

But that Sunday, I was back at the pulpit.

Marble floors. Wooden pews. High arched ceiling. The stained glass behind me. People in the pews leaning forward, attentive.

I sang. I told jokes. I got the room to say amen. Made me stand up tall and proud. Validated. Valued.

I had been doing it since 2014. Weaver every single Sunday, Sunday in and Sunday out. A pretty big savior complex. Hopeful optimism that I was going to rescue the church.

---

Two different rooms. Same feeling.

Both pulled people in. Both made me stand up tall. Both made me feel like the person I thought I was supposed to be.

One had stained glass behind it. The other had buckets of color and the kids bleary-eyed at six in the morning.

I didn't yet know which one was mine.

---

## Closing

The Saturdays kept coming. The flowers kept growing. We were a flagship vendor before long.

In February of the next year, I started sitting in my office watching a video about depression.
