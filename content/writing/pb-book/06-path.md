I texted fifty friends from the trailer in Pleasant Valley.

*hey its matt, hope you're doing well! I'm stable now and doing web design. You know anybody?*

It was hard and scary. But it pulled me out.

I had been silent for six months. No Facebook posts. No calls or texts. Just withdrew. Disappeared. I had wanted to reconnect the whole time. I had a community, a network. I did not want to lose that, but I felt like I had.

After basking in rejection, I started asking for referrals.

Getting hung up on sucks.

---

The path was not glamorous.

I took a job at the City of Anniston farmers market — same market where Heather and I had once been a flagship vendor. Now I was the one running it. Karla Eden was my boss. The first Saturday I worked the market, the other vendors looked at me sideways. The flower-farm guy was now the market guy. I do not know how it landed for them.

It was a step.

I built a website for a friend. Then another. Then I figured out the real problem was not the websites — it was the message. Then I built a framework around that.

One shoe. Then the other.

---

Last week I went to a Chamber of Commerce meetup at Called Coffee.

After a few conversations I sat down to take a break. A woman across the room waved. Sarah, my wife's boss at the museum. A flower patron not long ago. I walked over. Karla Eden and a couple of others were debating who I was.

*"That's Matt from the flower farm."*

They said it was sad that it ended. I said I was still sad too.

Three coffee meetups this week.

---

I started wondering if building a business could be good for my mental and emotional health.

That was a sentence I would not have written six months earlier. Building a business had ended my mental and emotional health a year before that. The framework I am writing about in this book started in this period — when I noticed that having clients to call back, websites to ship, profiles to write, was the actual work of getting better. Not the meditation. Not the medication. Not the candle. Those were the floor. The work was the work.

---

After the texts, I finally started to feel ready to write.

I reached out to Samuel at Aquality Farms. To Jean at Mom-to-Go. After writing and sharing the profiles, people read and commented, and that felt really good. I needed some wins. This was a way to win before I had any clients. It felt like I was connecting again. I had something to talk about.

I did not have a pulpit anymore. But I still needed a place to process publicly. The pulpit used to be that, but it was limited. Now I write freely.
