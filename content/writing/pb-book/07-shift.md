This Easter was our second Sunday visiting Jacksonville First Methodist. We sat in the back row.

Andy was the pastor — an old friend from my Weaver UMC pastoring days. He had been there since June.

During the service, he asked if anyone had been invited to help serve communion. No one had.

He looked me square in the eye and invited me by name.

*"I'm going to ask my friend Matt Headley to help me serve communion."*

I was holding the bulletin.

After months of not attending church at all. After feeling like maybe I had lost my faith. After wondering if resurrection was even possible, let alone beginning.

I set the bulletin in the pew and walked to the front of the room, next to the communion table.

---

The cup in my hands, the bread in his.

Looking in the eyes of the people who came forward. The words came the way they always had.

*"The blood of Jesus, shed for you."*

After the service, Andy walked up to me. Said *"Thank you. Sorry I sprung that on you. You're a talented musician and a great pastor."* We agreed to get coffee soon.

---

A friend met me for coffee on Easter week.

She has bipolar too. We sat by the window — my request. Late morning light in her eyes.

*"If you don't mind me wearing my sunglasses while we talk."*

She paused.

*"Your ministry will be stronger because of what you've been through. Sharing your story can help others and help you. It helped me…"*

I had thought that was true. Had been told so. Had felt it after my first recovery. I did not know if I would ever return to ministry.

*"You can be in ministry without working at a church."*

I knew that. But it was different hearing it from a former parishioner.

---

The shift had not arrived as a dramatic moment.

The bedtime candle had kept burning the entire time — through the manic year, through the hospital, through Cropwell, through the trailer's first Christmas, through Easter at Anniston First when I was wearing purple pants and a pink tie and was completely unwell, through the months of withdrawal, through the texts to fifty friends.

We had not given that up. I had not wanted to give up on my kids. I had not wanted to give up on my faith.

The shift was Andy looking me in the eye and saying my name. The shift was sitting at a coffee table with someone whose serotonin was store-bought looking at me and telling me what I had already half-believed.

This is the chapter most readers will recognize before any of the others. The moment is not the rock bottom. It is not the comeback speech. It is the ordinary Tuesday when you stopped explaining yourself the old way and just said the plain thing — and someone you trusted said it back.

The framework I write about in this book lives in the moment after that — when the message starts to work because the work has started to work.

"The shift, I think, was maybe around January when Christmas was over, and the kids were starting school for the first time in public school. And I realized I've gotta do something with my days. I can't sit at home and just pedal to my thumbs, and I did that for a while. I've been playing solitaire for months to try to distract myself, but I decide maybe I'll just learn how to — after applying for jobs for several weeks and getting zero traction, no callbacks, no nothing, I thought, maybe I'll just take a class on coding and see where it goes. So I joined the Zero to Mastery program, and I do that for a while. I watch the webinar sitting there at my computer desk, and I do some web development and learn HTML and CSS."

"Then talks with my brother, one of those walks on the phone. And he tells me about how he barely looks at code anymore, and he uses AI to vibe code. And I start listening to podcasts about it. I listen to podcasts about web development and building your own business and selling websites and about vibe coding and the pros and cons of that, and I decided to start doing it. And that's when I kinda start developing the seed of Headley Web and SEO."

"The moment has shifted for me when I'm watching Lee Blue, and he's talking about doing the local legends project. And he's standing there in his blue office, and he's talking about doing basically free articles or profiles on local business owners as a way to passively generate leads, like a lead magnet or lead funnel. And I'm like, wow. That's smart. And so I start thinking about doing that. And I felt inspired, and I felt energized by that."

"I'm sitting at my desk, and then I'm walking up and down the street with my sweatpants listening to the podcasts."

"Sitting at the desk is when I discovered him, and then I started just copying all the transcripts from his YouTube videos and watching him and dumping them into AI and building a business plan."

"I leaned in."

"His energy and his optimism and his encouragement and his, like, you can do this kind of mentality. I'm like, I want that."

"He's doing the story brand method. He's doing plain spoken blueprint before I even had it, and it's funny — his name Lee Blue and I landed on plain spoken blueprint."

"I'm also listening to Donald Miller podcasts while I'm driving back and forth to my daughter's dance lessons, and I'm reading Donald Miller's Building a Story Brand book while I'm sitting at the car dealership trying to get my wife's car fixed because it's a lemon. We bought it when I had to sell the truck, and we bought the CRV. And I was so devastated."

"I copy paste, man. I copy paste."
