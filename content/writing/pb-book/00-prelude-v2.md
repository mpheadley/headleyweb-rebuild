## Opening

I had been driving since seven that morning.

Out of the trailer in Pleasant Valley, twenty minutes south to Oxford for a BNI visitor day, then back north to Go Social Media to pick up the totes I had printed for Heather to sell at the farmers market. Chief Ladiga Trail totes. Model City totes. They sat in the back seat in a stack.

It was May seventh. The day after my forty-second birthday.

I had just told a room of business owners about a messaging clarity framework I was building. I did not have a name for it yet. Sitting in the Go Social Media parking lot now, the totes in the back, I was working it out.

Different things. Trying different things. The framework wanted a name that was not *story* something, not *brand* something. Plain. The opposite of clever.

*Clever confuses. Clarity sells.*

I texted Heather the tagline and three brand-name options. The one I kept circling back to was Plainspoken Blueprint.

She wrote back: *I love it.*

I drove home talking out loud, working the idea through. By the time I got to the driveway, I knew what I was going to do next.

The trailer was where we lived now. The farm had sold while I was in the hospital the previous fall. My parents brought the trailer over and parked it on a piece of land on my in-laws' property in Pleasant Valley. We moved in after Thanksgiving.

My desk inside it was my son's old desk. The good one we'd bought a year before the farm sale was too big to fit through the door of the cubby. The cubby is a narrow space at the back of the trailer with a water heater behind the chair. The water heater blows cold air when it runs. Papers on either side of the monitor.

Heather was at work. The kids were at school. I sat down.

---

I started typing the eight movements of the hero's journey out from memory. Actor. Wound. Mask. Scar. Light. Path. Shift. Home.

I got to the Wound and stopped.

Heartbreak. Loss. I closed my eyes.

I had been trying to outpace, outrun, outsmart, outmaneuver my brokenness for years. The wound was the thing I had been refusing to look at the whole time I was preaching about how to look at things.

I kept going. I got to the Guide.

The guide that walks with a limp. The guide with scars in his hands.

I said it out loud.

Jesus.

That was the thing the keyboard moment broke open. Not the framework. The fact that the framework was already inside me. I had been living it for six years without naming it.

I had lost a farm. I had resigned from a twenty-year ministry career in a manic episode. I had spent ten days in a psychiatric ward at UAB. I had been diagnosed bipolar. I had moved from a 1832 farmhouse to a trailer on my in-laws' land. I had spent the fall of 2025 inside, not going outside, not picking up the phone. I had gained thirty-five pounds and watched a lot of YouTube and eventually started walking outside every morning to try to get my life back.

The framework in this book was built from that ground.

I do not write as a clarity expert. I write as a person who spent twenty years trying to be clever and watched it stop working. I write as a person whose ministry career ended because I could not see clearly. Whose farm closed partly because the messaging never landed. Whose business pitch took four versions before the fourth one — the plain one — got a callback.

This book is built on that pattern.

Eight movements. Each one a moment from my own story. Each one a step in a framework I use with small business owners who do not need another marketing book — they need a clear message that closes deals.

I do not believe most small businesses have a marketing problem. I believe they have a clarity problem. The framework here is what I learned to do about that after fifteen years of losing the room from a pulpit and four years of trying to sell flowers from a roadside kiosk and four months of building a SaaS app suite that nobody could explain back to me.

The acronym is SEED. See. Engage. Equip. Deliver.

The eight movements run underneath it: Actor, Wound, Mask, Scar, Light, Path, Shift, Home. My story is one version of those movements. Your story is another.
