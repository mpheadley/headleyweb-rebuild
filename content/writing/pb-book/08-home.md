We bought the Bloom Bar in 2024.

A floral cart, custom-built, $7,000. We used it once before the farm closed. It sat in the grass at our place in Pleasant Valley for almost a year. A symbol of a dream that did not make it.

I did not get rid of it.

This October it becomes the centerpiece of The Aisle, the first bridal expo we are hosting at Anniston Museums and Gardens. The venue is free — Heather is the Program Manager there. The Bloom Bar will be set up where two hundred and fifty couples are going to walk past it on October eighteenth.

Same flowers. Same hands. Same NE Alabama.

---

I have been silent for six months. No Facebook posts. No calls or texts. Just withdrew. Disappeared. And missed people the whole time. I wanted to reconnect. I had a community, a network. I did not want to lose that, but I felt like I had.

I walk up and down Little John Road in Pleasant Valley a few times a day. I walk past farms and gardens and fences and houses. Things people have that I had. That I built. That I was planning to build. There are still hard feelings about that. Heather and I talk about it when we walk.

I still hesitate before looking at the farms and gardens. I look at them anyway.

---

Southern Legends was conceived as a top-of-funnel strategy for Headley Web.

Giving free value for business owners, hoping the goodwill would help me find clients. But something was blocking me. Writing about business owners that were succeeding where I had failed. The comparison and jealousy were too hard. So I pocketed the idea. Did cold calling instead.

No baggage with people who did not know me. Easy.

Getting hung up on sucks.

After basking in rejection, I started asking for referrals. I texted fifty friends. I reached out to Samuel at Aquality Farms. To Jean at Mom-to-Go. After writing and sharing the profiles, people read and commented, and that felt really good. I needed some wins. This was a way to win before I had any clients. It felt like I was connecting again. I had something to talk about.

I do not have a pulpit anymore. But I still need a place to process publicly. The pulpit used to be that, but it was limited. Now I write freely.

---

I kept wondering, though: does an honest, messy journal belong here? On a site I built to profile local business owners? Will it damage my credibility for getting business?

Why do I need to publish this? Is not a private journal good enough?

I posted anyway.

Some clients could be turned off by my history. Bipolar. Hospitalization. Business failure. Maybe those are not the best clients anyway.

Maybe being broken is what makes the profiles work.

Both the journal and the profiles, on the same domain.

---

A friend of mine — the one who has had bipolar since her twenties — told me that ministry can happen without a building. She said it twice. I made her say it twice because I needed it twice. She said *"You can be in ministry without working at a church."*

I think she meant *home* is not the place you arrive at. It is the way you start moving differently in the place you were already in.

I have the same wife I had on the farm. The same kids. The same Little John Road. The same trail crossing on Highway 204. The same first Methodist Anniston I am no longer at. The same Anniston Museums and Gardens where Heather works. The same flowers Heather grew before, in arrangements Heather still makes. The same Bloom Bar.
