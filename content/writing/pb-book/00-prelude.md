I built this framework after a few hard years.

I have bipolar disorder. I lost a farm. I spent time in a psychiatric hospital. I gained thirty-five pounds and watched a lot of YouTube and eventually started walking outside every morning to try to get my life back.

That's what recovery looked like for me. And building again. This book is part of it.

---

I had a friend who had been bipolar since her twenties. When we finally sat down for coffee, she already knew her story. I was still learning mine. Late morning light through the window. She asked if I'd mind if she kept her sunglasses on. She told me my experience would make me more effective in ministry.

I wasn't sure I believed her.

"You don't have to be working at a church to do ministry," she said.

I didn't know what to do with that for a long time. I had been a Methodist pastor since 2014. The pulpit was the room I knew how to fill. When I lost it, I assumed I was losing the work.

The work was not the pulpit. The work was the noticing. The way a person leans in when you put words around something they already feel. That doesn't stop happening when you leave the building.

---

The framework in this book is not the work of a clarity expert.

It is the work of someone who spent twenty years saying things in ways that did not land. Sermons that lost the room. Newsletters that did not get read. Marketing copy I wrote for the farm that meant nothing to anyone walking past the kiosk. A business pitch I tried four times before the fourth time, when I stopped explaining and just said the plain thing.

I am not the smartest person in your industry. I am the person who has watched what happens when clever fails.

This book is what I have learned to do instead.

---

Most messaging frameworks are built for corporate marketing teams. People with budgets, time, focus groups, departments. They ask you to spend three days in a workshop or read three hundred pages of theory.

You do not have three days. You have a lunch break and a notebook.

So this book is built for that.

Eight movements. Each one a single shift in how you say what you do. Each one anchored to a moment from my own story so you know the framework was built by someone who needed it badly enough to test it on himself first. The personal arc and the methodology run side by side. You can read one without the other. You will get more from reading both.
