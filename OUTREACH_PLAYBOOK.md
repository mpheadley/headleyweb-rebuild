## Part 1 — Market Context

### Main Street Anniston Programs (AL Spark + GETUP)

Both programs run through Main Street Anniston (mainstreetanniston.com) — not Main Street Alabama statewide.

**AL Spark** — existing businesses. $1,000 SBA-funded digital marketing grants. Recipients complete training and receive grant money to spend on implementation. 2024 cohort: 7 Anniston businesses. Pre-qualified prospects with budget already allocated.

**GETUP** — startups and early-stage founders. Free 8-week business training (accounting, digital marketing, location selection, etc.). Curriculum taught by community experts. Pitch competition: $5K–$10K for downtown launches. Pop-up retail via Small Box Shop.

**Strategic relevance:** AL Spark graduates need someone to spend their grant with — no local web agency is positioned as the go-to implementation partner. GETUP is a teaching opportunity: get in front of every new startup founder in the cohort as the digital marketing expert before they hire anyone.

**The pitch when you call:**
> "I'm a local web designer based in Jacksonville. I saw that you run AL Spark and GETUP and I'd love to find out how I can be a resource — either as someone grant recipients can work with, or as a presenter for the digital marketing piece of GETUP. Who would I talk to about that?"

**CRM:** Contact added (ID 283), follow-up Monday 2026-04-21. See call script above.

**Main Street Alabama (statewide)** — secondary contact after building the local relationship. They run AL Spark in other cities (Oxford, Gadsden, etc.) — can expand Matt's reach once the Anniston relationship is established.

## Part 3 — Cold Walk-In Strategy

### Objective

One day per week walking into local service businesses to introduce Headley Web & SEO, leave behind materials, and establish initial awareness. No hard sell. Plant seeds.

### Target Profile

- Service companies (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, landscaping, pest control, etc.)
- Located in Northeast Alabama (Calhoun County and surrounding areas)
- Have weak, outdated, or no website
- Listed on Google Business Profile

### Lead Sourcing

Use the Google Business profile scraper to identify targets before each walk-in day:

1. Run scraper on the target city or area (e.g., Lincoln, Oxford, Anniston)
2. Filter for businesses with no website or low review counts
3. Build a short target list for that day — prioritize geographic clusters to save drive time
4. Note the business name, address, and owner name if available

### Before You Start — Have Something to Show

Don't do walk-ins with the free build offer until you have at least one live client site to pull up on your phone. Without it, you're asking a cold stranger to trust an unknown quantity.

**The trigger:** Ship Between Worlds (Giovanna's site). That's the case study. Once it's live, you walk in with a real URL, a real client you can name, and optionally a testimonial quote.

### The Approach

The whole visit should take under 3 minutes. You are not closing a deal — you are making a name stick.

Tested method (from Thomas Briskey, Vector Security): walk in cold, ask to leave info, converse if they're open.

1. Walk in friendly and relaxed — no pitch energy
2. Introduce yourself in one sentence
3. Ask what they do — make them talk
4. Hand over the brochure
5. Ask permission to follow up
6. Exit without pressure

**What to say:**
> "Hi, I'm Matt — I do websites and Google for local service businesses. Just dropping by to introduce myself." *(hand over brochure)* "What kind of work do you all do?"

**Permission ask:**
> "Mind if I follow up with you this week?"

If yes — get their name and number or email. That evening, run their site through headleyweb.com/audit. The tool auto-generates a PDF report and emails it to them. Add one personal observation when you send the follow-up.

**Exit line (if they're not open to follow-up):**
> "No worries — the QR on there will pull up a free checkup of your site anytime."

### Leave-Behind Materials

- Business card (already ordered)
- One-pager: your name, what you do, one before-and-after example, contact info, QR to free audit tool

### Talking Points — Free Build Offer

> "I'm building a few free sites right now as case studies — normally runs $800 to $1,200. If you're interested I'd love to take a look at what you've got."

**Handling follow-up questions:**
- **"Why free?"** — "I'm building my portfolio in this area and I want the before-and-after data. You get a real site, I get a case study. Fair trade."
- **"What's the catch?"** — "Three months in I'll show you what moved. After that it's $49 to $199 a month to keep it running. No pressure, no contract."
- **"We already have a website"** — "Mind if I take a quick look?" Pull it up on your phone. If it's bad, you'll both see it without you having to say a word.

### PE Rollup Talking Point

Use this when an owner says "I'll get to the website eventually" or "we've been doing fine on referrals":

> "You saw that Dunn's got bought, right? That company has a national marketing team behind them now. The businesses that get their digital presence locked in before the rollups take over the map pack are going to have a real advantage — because they're still the local face, just with a better digital system."

Don't lead with this. Use it when they bring up competition, slow months, or "the market's changing."

### What NOT To Do

- Do not lead with the Southern Legends profile offer on first contact — save that for warm follow-ups
- Do not overstay or turn a drop-off into an unsolicited pitch
- Do not badmouth their current site — frame it as an opportunity, not a problem
- Do not leave without getting at least the owner's name if possible

### If the Owner Is Not There

- Be genuinely friendly to the receptionist or admin
- Ask for the owner's name
- Leave your one-pager clipped to your business card
- Add a short handwritten note if it feels right

### Follow-Up

- Wait 2–3 weeks before a light follow-up (return visit or phone call)
- Southern Legends profile offer can be introduced here if they weren't ready for web design

### Tracking

After each walk-in day, log to CRM:

| Date | Business Name | City | Owner Name | Website Status | Left Materials | Follow-Up Date | Notes |
|------|---------------|------|------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|-------|

### Cadence

- **One full day per week** dedicated to walk-ins
- Target **15–20 businesses per day** depending on proximity
- Use scraper data to plan efficient geographic routes

### Prospect Channels (ranked by conversion likelihood)

**Channel 1 — Orphaned clients (HIGHEST priority)**
Local web designers/agencies that have closed, gone inactive, or abandoned clients.
- Search for defunct local agencies (dead sites, expired domains, no GBP activity)

## Part 5 — Discovery Workflow (Pre-Meeting Research)

> Run this the day before any discovery call or in-person meeting. Estimated time: 30–60 minutes.
> Captures baseline "before" numbers that become the case study later.

### Step 0 — What You Need to Start
- Prospect's business name and website URL
- Their industry / service type
- Meeting date/time (for cheatsheet date stamp)

### Step 1 — Audit Their Website

Run `fetch-site-audit.py` against their URL. Captures:
- Mobile + desktop PageSpeed scores
- Meta description present/malformed
- Viewport meta tag (mobile-friendly)
- Canonical tag
- JSON-LD schema (LocalBusiness)
- Pages indexed (`site:` query)

**Key numbers to pull out:**
- LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) — the headline "before" number
- Mobile PageSpeed score (out of 100)

> Mobile score matters most for local service businesses — lead with that.
> If rate-limited by PageSpeed API, wait a few minutes and retry desktop separately.

### Step 2 — StoryBrand Messaging Audit

Run their homepage through headleyweb.com/quiz. Captures:
- Hero / Problem / Guide / Plan / CTA / Stakes / Messaging scores
- Overall grade (out of 30)
- Key copy problems

**What to look for:**
- Does their hero headline name *who they help* or just describe their business?
- Is the main CTA visible above the fold?
- Is there any problem/pain language, or is it all features?

### Step 3 — Keyword Ranking Research

Search for their 2–3 most important keywords:
1. `[service] [city/state]` — their primary local keyword
2. `[niche] [industry vertical] [state]` — any industry-specific modifier

**What to capture:**
- Are they appearing at all?
- Who is ranking in positions 1–5?
- Is there a Map Pack? Are they in it?

### Step 4 — Competitor Analysis

For each top-ranking competitor, note:
- Why they outrank the prospect (state-specific pages, explicit B2B language, content volume, GBP authority)
- One specific thing the prospect could do to close the gap

**Three questions per competitor:**
1. What do they have that your prospect doesn't? (pages, keywords, schema)
2. Who is their copy written for?
3. What's the easiest thing to copy/beat?

### Step 5 — Google Business Profile Check

Search their business name + city in Google Maps. Check:
- [ ] GBP exists?
- [ ] Claimed or unclaimed?
- [ ] Star rating + review count
- [ ] Categories set correctly?
- [ ] Photos present?
- [ ] Website linked?

An unclaimed GBP with any stars is a high-urgency, low-effort talking point.

### Step 6 — Social / Platform Scan (2 minutes)

- Facebook page: follower count, last post date, whether services match the website
- LinkedIn (if B2B): company page exists?
- Yelp / BBB / industry directories: any profiles that need updating?

### Step 7 — Populate the Cheatsheet

Update `docs/meeting-cheatsheet.html` with findings:
- Fill in actual PageSpeed scores (mobile + desktop)
- Check/uncheck technical audit boxes
- Add competitor names and their ranking advantages
- Note GBP status

### Step 8 — Baseline Metrics Doc

Fill in `BASELINE_METRICS.md`:
- All Lighthouse scores
- Keyword ranking status (verified + date)
- Competitor table
- GBP status

This becomes your case study "before" numbers.

### Step 9 — Generate All Print PDFs

```bash
bash generate-pdfs.sh
```

Produces:
- `docs/meeting-cheatsheet.pdf` — your reference doc, printed and brought to the meeting
- `docs/[client]-next-steps.pdf` — the branded Headley Web leave-behind, handed to the client

**Brand colors:** All HTML docs use Headley Web brand colors — terracotta `#E07B3C`, sage `#6B8F71`, dark `#1C2826`. Always copy existing templates (`meeting-cheatsheet.html`, `leave-behind.html`) rather than creating from scratch.

### Quick-Reference: Talking Points by Finding

| Finding | Talking Point |
|---------|--------------|
| LCP > 3s | "Your site loads in Xs. That's slow enough that Google penalizes it in rankings — and visitors bounce." |
| Viewport missing | "Your site isn't technically mobile-friendly. Google uses mobile-first indexing, so this hurts your ranking directly." |
| No JSON-LD schema | "Google can't confirm what your business does or where you're located. Schema markup fixes that." |
| GBP unclaimed | "Your Google listing exists but it's unclaimed — meaning someone else could claim it. We fix that immediately." |
| Not ranking for primary keyword | "Right now, when [buyer] searches for [keyword], you don't appear. [Competitor] does. Here's why." |
| StoryBrand < 15/30 | "Your homepage talks about your business. The new site talks to the person who's going to write you a check." |
